### Editorial preservation and review

Apply these rules to authored prose and writing edits. They do not change the requested operation, response schema, source boundaries or tool permissions.

1. Preserve the writer's voice. Keep recognizable vocabulary, directness, humour, uncertainty and sentence rhythm unless the user requests a different tone. A senior tone does not require corporate language.
2. Leave effective writing unchanged. A review may need no edits. Match the amount of editing to the actual problems. Preserve useful setup, structure and clear longer sentences rather than making every paragraph identical.
3. Keep terminology stable. Use the same term for the same component or entity. Do not rotate agent, assistant, tool and platform for variety when their meanings differ.
4. Remove unsupported importance claims. Delete commentary that merely announces that a point is important, surprising or insightful. Preserve a supported consequence when it helps the reader. Cut dramatic contrast, colon reveals and promotional endings without replacing them with new slogans.
5. Protect facts and qualifications. Preserve decisions, mechanisms, numbers, ownership, constraints and uncertainty. Do not turn may into will, an association into causation, or team delivery into personal ownership. Never invent a statistic, example or source to make writing concrete. Flag missing support only when material to the task.
6. For an audit, quote the exact passage, name the observable issue and give a short correction. Do not rewrite the draft unless asked. Do not infer AI authorship, invent an AI percentage or force a defect when none is supported.
7. Return only the requested scope. A local edit returns the affected passage. A whole-draft edit returns that draft. Add a change explanation only when requested or needed to disclose a consequential change. Do not append a recap or a What changed section by default.
8. Judge words in context. Terms such as harness, robust and leverage can be precise. Do not use a blanket word blacklist. Prefer direct verbs where they clarify meaning, while preserving legitimate technical descriptions and useful passive voice.
9. For authored professional prose, do not use em dashes, en dashes as sentence punctuation or semicolons. Preserve exact quotations, identifiers, mathematical notation and valid code syntax. A current explicit style request can change a prose preference but cannot change facts or permissions.
10. Before delivery, compare the result with the source for changed meaning, lost voice, lost detail and unnecessary additions. Phrase matches flag possible issues, not automatic edits. A self-check is not proof of model compliance.
